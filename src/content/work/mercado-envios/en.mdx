## Problem & context

Mercado Envios is Mercado Livre's logistics operation. Its warehouses run on Mercury, Mercado Livre's own warehouse management system (WMS), across fulfillment centers, cross-docking hubs and last-mile operations in Latin America. Operators on the floor work through Mercury's screens: inbound and outbound, inventory, and every step a package takes in between.

When packages land at a warehouse, someone has to sort them before they can move on. That step needs a screen that is fast and clear for the people doing it all shift, across every country Mercury runs in.

## Role

Front-end Engineer from September 2020 to July 2021, on a team of three engineers, a tech lead and a manager.

## What I built

- **The landing-packages sort page.** I built the Mercury page operators use to sort landing packages, used across Latin America.
- **The BFF behind it.** I helped create the backend-for-frontend that serves this page: one API shaped around what the screen needs, so the front end doesn't stitch together calls to several warehouse services.
- **The RFC for it.** I documented the data flow of the landing-packages page in an RFC, so the team and the services it depended on could agree on the contract in writing.

## Approach

Mercury's front end is a set of high-availability micro-frontend applications, each shipping on its own. Over my time on the team I contributed to 450+ production releases across these applications, which have to stay up while warehouses operate.

## Challenges & trade-offs

- **A BFF is one more service to run.** It is worth it when the screen's needs differ from the services' shape: the page gets exactly the data it uses, and changes to the services stay behind one boundary.
- **Write it down first.** An RFC slows the start a little and saves time later, because the discussion happens on the document instead of in review.

## Results & evidence

- The landing-packages sort page, used in Mercury across Latin America.
- Its BFF, and an RFC documenting its data flow.
- Contributions to 450+ production releases across Mercury's micro-frontends.

## Learnings

- **Design for the person on the floor.** Operators use the same screen all shift. Small delays and unclear states cost more there than anywhere else.
- **Contracts before code.** Agreeing on the data flow in writing made building the page and its BFF the easy part.
